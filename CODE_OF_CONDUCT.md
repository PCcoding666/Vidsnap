# Code of Conduct

## Our standards

Treat every contributor with respect. We build in public:

- Disagree about code, not people. Challenge ideas with evidence — benchmarks, traces, reproducible examples — and assume good faith. Being wrong is not a violation; staying respectful is the standard.
- Preserve sources. When reusing code, docs, diagrams, or benchmark profiles from others, keep licenses and attribution intact: name the author, link the origin, and never strip provenance when adapting or porting work.
- Write for the long term. Keep discussions on-topic and searchable, and summarize conclusions so latecomers can follow.
- Give and accept review gracefully. Feedback targets artifacts, not authors.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, personal attacks, or insults.
- Trolling, deliberate derailing, or bad-faith arguments.
- Sexualized language or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without consent.
- Sharing credentials, media, datasets, or other artifacts the repository guidelines prohibit committing.
- Removing or misrepresenting attribution or license terms of third-party work.
- Weaponizing the reporting process with knowingly false claims.

## Scope

Applies to this repository and its spaces — code, issues, pull requests, discussions, docs, and official project channels — plus public statements claiming to represent the project. Platform abuse (spam, impersonation, account-level harassment) is additionally subject to GitHub's Terms of Service.

## Reporting and enforcement

- To report a violation, ask the repository maintainers for a private channel — do not post incident details publicly. Share what happened once a private route is open.
- For abuse that lives on the platform itself (spam, impersonation, harassment across GitHub), use GitHub's built-in reporting tools on the content or account.
- Reports are handled confidentially to the extent possible. Maintainers investigate, decide an outcome, and record the enforcement action. Reporter identities are not published.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers apply consequences proportionate to severity and recurrence:

1. **Warning** — private, for a first minor violation; expectations restated.
2. **Temporary exclusion** — repeated or serious violations; restricted from issues, pull requests, or discussions.
3. **Permanent removal** — harassment, doxxing, or sustained abuse.

Moderation actions (removing comments, closing threads, blocking) must cite the violated standard. Anyone affected may ask maintainers to reconsider once.
